People ask AI about health and medicine more than almost any other topic. Common questions include symptoms of common illnesses. The common cold causes runny nose, sore throat, cough, sneezing, and mild fatigue. It is caused by a virus, so antibiotics do not help. Treatment includes rest, fluids, honey for cough, and over the counter pain relievers like acetaminophen or ibuprofen. The flu, or influenza, causes sudden high fever, body aches, chills, severe fatigue, dry cough, and headache. Flu symptoms are more severe than cold symptoms. Antiviral drugs can help if taken early. The COVID 19 virus causes fever, cough, loss of taste or smell, shortness of breath, fatigue, and body aches. Some people have no symptoms. Vaccines reduce severity. Long COVID can cause persistent fatigue and brain fog. Strep throat causes severe sore throat, pain when swallowing, fever, and swollen lymph nodes, but usually no cough. It requires antibiotics to prevent complications. Allergies cause sneezing, runny or stuffy nose, itchy watery eyes, and sometimes hives. Antihistamines like loratadine or cetirizine help. Anaphylaxis is a severe allergic reaction causing difficulty breathing, swelling of the throat, and drop in blood pressure. It requires immediate epinephrine injection. Migraine headaches cause throbbing pain often on one side of the head, along with nausea, sensitivity to light and sound. Some people see auras before the migraine. Treatment includes triptans, anti nausea drugs, and avoiding triggers. Tension headaches cause dull, pressing pain on both sides of the head, often from stress or muscle tension. Over the counter pain relievers and relaxation help. Sinus headaches cause pressure in the forehead, cheeks, and behind the eyes, often with nasal congestion. Decongestants and saline spray help. People ask about fever. A fever is a temporary increase in body temperature, usually above 38 degrees Celsius or 100.4 degrees Fahrenheit. Low grade fevers help the immune system fight infection. High fevers above 39.5 Celsius or 103 Fahrenheit may need medical attention. Infants with fever need immediate care. People ask about blood pressure. Normal blood pressure is below 120 over 80 millimeters of mercury. High blood pressure or hypertension is consistently 130 over 80 or higher. It often has no symptoms but increases risk of heart attack and stroke. Low blood pressure or hypotension can cause dizziness and fainting. People ask about cholesterol. LDL or low density lipoprotein is bad cholesterol that clogs arteries. HDL or high density lipoprotein is good cholesterol that removes bad cholesterol. Total cholesterol should be below 200 milligrams per deciliter. Statins are drugs that lower cholesterol. People ask about diabetes. Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition where the pancreas produces little or no insulin. It usually starts in childhood. Type 2 diabetes occurs when the body becomes resistant to insulin or the pancreas cannot produce enough. It is associated with obesity and lifestyle. Gestational diabetes occurs during pregnancy. Symptoms include frequent urination, excessive thirst, unexplained weight loss, blurred vision, and slow healing wounds. Blood sugar is measured with a glucose meter. Hemoglobin A1C measures average blood sugar over three months. People ask about mental health. Depression causes persistent sadness, loss of interest in activities, changes in appetite and sleep, fatigue, feelings of worthlessness, and thoughts of death or suicide. Treatment includes therapy such as cognitive behavioral therapy and medications such as SSRIs like fluoxetine or sertraline. Anxiety disorders cause excessive worry, restlessness, muscle tension, difficulty concentrating, and sleep problems. Generalized anxiety disorder involves worry about many things. Panic attacks cause sudden intense fear, racing heart, sweating, trembling, shortness of breath, and feeling of losing control. Social anxiety disorder is fear of social situations. Treatment includes therapy and medications like SSRIs or benzodiazepines for short term use. Bipolar disorder causes episodes of mania or hypomania with elevated mood, increased energy, decreased need for sleep, racing thoughts, and impulsive behavior, alternating with depressive episodes. Lithium and other mood stabilizers are treatments. PTSD or post traumatic stress disorder occurs after a traumatic event, causing intrusive memories, nightmares, flashbacks, avoidance of reminders, negative mood changes, and hypervigilance. Therapy includes EMDR, eye movement desensitization and reprocessing, and prolonged exposure therapy. ADHD or attention deficit hyperactivity disorder causes inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity. It can persist into adulthood. Treatment includes stimulant medications like methylphenidate and amphetamines, and behavioral strategies. OCD or obsessive compulsive disorder causes unwanted intrusive thoughts called obsessions and repetitive behaviors called compulsions performed to reduce anxiety from the obsessions. Exposure and response prevention therapy is effective. Eating disorders include anorexia nervosa, restricting food intake leading to dangerously low body weight and intense fear of gaining weight, bulimia nervosa with cycles of binge eating followed by purging through vomiting or laxatives, and binge eating disorder with episodes of eating large amounts without purging. Treatment involves therapy and nutritional counseling. People ask about sleep. Adults need seven to nine hours of sleep per night. Insomnia is difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early. Sleep hygiene includes consistent bedtimes, dark and quiet room, avoiding screens before bed, limiting caffeine and alcohol, and relaxing before sleep. Sleep apnea causes breathing to stop and start during sleep, leading to loud snoring, gasping, and daytime fatigue. CPAP machines treat sleep apnea. Narcolepsy causes excessive daytime sleepiness and sudden sleep attacks. People ask about nutrition. Calories are units of energy. Weight loss occurs when calories consumed are less than calories burned. Weight gain occurs when calories consumed exceed calories burned. Basal metabolic rate is the calories burned at rest. Macronutrients are carbohydrates, proteins, and fats. Carbohydrates provide four calories per gram. They include sugars, starches, and fiber. Whole grains, fruits, and vegetables are healthy carbohydrates. Refined carbohydrates like white bread and sugary drinks are less healthy. Proteins provide four calories per gram. They are made of amino acids. Complete proteins contain all essential amino acids and come from animal sources like meat, fish, eggs, and dairy. Incomplete proteins come from plant sources like beans, lentils, nuts, and seeds. Combining plant proteins can provide all essential amino acids. Fats provide nine calories per gram. Unsaturated fats from olive oil, avocados, nuts, and fish are healthy. Saturated fats from red meat, butter, and coconut oil should be limited. Trans fats from processed foods are harmful and should be avoided entirely. Fiber is indigestible carbohydrate that helps digestion, lowers cholesterol, and controls blood sugar. Soluble fiber dissolves in water and is found in oats, beans, and apples. Insoluble fiber does not dissolve and is found in whole grains, nuts, and vegetables. Recommended fiber intake is 25 to 30 grams per day. Vitamins are organic compounds needed in small amounts. Vitamin A is for vision and immune function. Vitamin C is for collagen production and antioxidant function. Vitamin D is for calcium absorption and bone health, produced by sunlight exposure. Vitamin E is an antioxidant. Vitamin K is for blood clotting. B vitamins including B12, folate, and niacin are for energy metabolism and nerve function. Minerals are inorganic elements. Calcium is for bones and teeth. Iron is for red blood cells. Magnesium is for muscle and nerve function. Potassium is for blood pressure and heart function. Sodium is for fluid balance but excess raises blood pressure. Zinc is for immune function and wound healing. People ask about exercise. Aerobic exercise includes running, swimming, cycling, and walking. It improves cardiovascular health. Recommended at least 150 minutes of moderate intensity or 75 minutes of vigorous intensity per week. Strength training includes weight lifting, bodyweight exercises, and resistance bands. It builds muscle, increases metabolism, and strengthens bones. Recommended at least two days per week. Flexibility training includes stretching and yoga. It improves range of motion and reduces injury risk. Balance training is important for older adults to prevent falls. Warm up before exercise with light activity and dynamic stretching. Cool down after exercise with light activity and static stretching. Hydration before, during, and after exercise is important. Overtraining causes fatigue, decreased performance, and increased injury risk. Rest days are essential. People ask about first aid. For cuts and scrapes, clean with water, apply antibiotic ointment, and cover with bandage. For deep wounds that bleed heavily, apply direct pressure, elevate the wound, and seek medical attention. For burns, cool with running water for 10 to 20 minutes, cover with sterile nonstick bandage, and do not apply ice or butter. For sprains and strains, use RICE, rest, ice, compression, elevation. For choking, perform the Heimlich maneuver, abdominal thrusts. For a person who is unconscious and not breathing, start CPR, chest compressions at 100 to 120 per minute, pushing down at least 2 inches or 5 centimeters, allowing full chest recoil. For an adult, use two hands. For a child, use one hand. For an infant, use two fingers. Use an AED, automated external defibrillator, if available. For poisoning, call poison control. Do not induce vomiting unless instructed. For heat exhaustion, move to cool area, hydrate, apply cool cloths. For heat stroke, which includes confusion and loss of consciousness, call emergency services and cool the person immediately. For frostbite, warm the area slowly in warm water, not hot, and do not rub. People ask about pregnancy. Pregnancy lasts about 40 weeks from the last menstrual period, divided into three trimesters. First trimester is weeks 1 to 12, with symptoms including nausea, fatigue, breast tenderness, and frequent urination. Second trimester is weeks 13 to 26, with symptoms including reduced nausea, visible baby bump, and feeling fetal movements. Third trimester is weeks 27 to 40, with symptoms including back pain, shortness of breath, swelling in feet and ankles, and Braxton Hicks contractions. Prenatal vitamins containing folic acid prevent neural tube defects. Regular prenatal checkups monitor blood pressure, weight, and fetal growth. Ultrasounds check fetal development. Genetic screening tests are optional. Common discomforts include morning sickness, heartburn, constipation, leg cramps, and hemorrhoids. Warning signs during pregnancy include severe headache, vision changes, severe abdominal pain, heavy bleeding, decreased fetal movement, and fluid leakage. Labor signs include regular contractions, water breaking, and bloody show. Labor has three stages. First stage is cervical dilation. Second stage is pushing and delivery of baby. Third stage is delivery of placenta. Postpartum recovery includes vaginal bleeding called lochia, breast engorgement, perineal pain, and hormonal changes. Postpartum depression is serious and requires treatment. Breastfeeding provides nutrition and antibodies. Formula is a safe alternative. People ask about child development. Newborns, birth to 2 months, sleep most of the day, feed every 2 to 3 hours, and start to smile. Infants, 2 to 12 months, learn to roll over, sit, crawl, stand, and may take first steps. They say first words like mama or dada. Toddlers, 1 to 3 years, walk, run, climb, use simple sentences, and have tantrums. Preschoolers, 3 to 5 years, use more complex speech, count, recognize colors, and play with others. School age children, 5 to 12 years, develop reading and math skills, make friends, and understand rules. Adolescents, 13 to 18 years, go through puberty with physical changes, seek independence, and develop identity. Vaccination schedule includes hepatitis B, DTaP for diphtheria tetanus pertussis, Hib for Haemophilus influenzae type b, polio, pneumococcal, rotavirus, MMR for measles mumps rubella, varicella for chickenpox, hepatitis A, HPV for human papillomavirus, and meningococcal. People ask about relationships. Communication skills include active listening, repeating back what you heard, I statements like I feel hurt when you do not call, avoiding blame and criticism, expressing needs clearly, and compromise. Conflict resolution includes identifying the problem, listening to each other's perspective, brainstorming solutions, agreeing on a solution, and following up. Signs of healthy relationship include trust, honesty, respect, support, shared values, good communication, and conflict resolution. Signs of unhealthy relationship include control, jealousy, isolation from friends and family, criticism, blame, gaslighting which is making someone doubt their own reality, physical violence, sexual coercion, and threats. Domestic violence hotlines provide help. Marriage and long term relationships require work, maintenance, and sometimes counseling. Attachment styles from childhood affect adult relationships. Secure attachment leads to healthy relationships. Anxious attachment leads to fear of abandonment and clinginess. Avoidant attachment leads to fear of intimacy and emotional distance. Disorganized attachment leads to chaotic relationships. Love languages include words of affirmation, acts of service, receiving gifts, quality time, and physical touch. People have different primary love languages. Friendships require reciprocity, trust, and shared interests. Loneliness is harmful to health. Social connections improve longevity and well being. People ask about parenting. Authoritative parenting, high warmth and high control, leads to best outcomes. Authoritarian parenting, low warmth and high control, leads to obedient but less happy children. Permissive parenting, high warmth and low control, leads to children with poor self regulation. Neglectful parenting, low warmth and low control, leads to worst outcomes. Positive discipline includes natural consequences, logical consequences, time outs, and explaining rules, not physical punishment. Spanking is associated with negative outcomes. Setting consistent limits, praising good behavior, modeling desired behavior, and listening to children are effective. Sleep training methods include cry it out, Ferber method, and no tears method. Potty training readiness signs include staying dry for longer periods, interest in the toilet, and ability to pull pants up and down. Screen time recommendations for children under 18 months avoid screens except video chat. For 18 to 24 months, introduce high quality programming with parent watching. For 2 to 5 years, limit to one hour per day of high quality programming. For 6 years and older, set consistent limits. Common childhood illnesses include ear infections, croup, hand foot and mouth disease, roseola, fifth disease, and RSV or respiratory syncytial virus. When to call pediatrician includes fever in infant under 3 months, fever over 40 Celsius or 104 Fahrenheit, difficulty breathing, lethargy, inconsolable crying, dehydration signs like fewer wet diapers, and rash with fever. People ask about food and cooking. Food safety includes washing hands, separating raw meat from other foods, cooking to safe internal temperatures, and refrigerating leftovers within two hours. Safe minimum internal temperatures are 63 Celsius or 145 Fahrenheit for whole cuts of beef pork lamb and veal, 71 Celsius or 160 Fahrenheit for ground meats, 74 Celsius or 165 Fahrenheit for poultry, and 60 Celsius or 140 Fahrenheit for fish. Common cooking techniques include sautéing in a pan with a small amount of oil over medium high heat, roasting in an oven at high heat, baking at moderate heat, braising browning then simmering in liquid, boiling in water at 100 Celsius or 212 Fahrenheit, simmering just below boiling, steaming over boiling water, grilling over direct heat, and frying in hot oil. Knife skills include the claw grip for holding food, pinch grip for holding the knife, chopping, dicing, mincing, julienne for thin matchsticks, and chiffonade for cutting herbs and leafy greens. Baking is precise. Measurements must be accurate. Leavening agents include yeast, baking powder, baking soda, and eggs. Gluten is a protein in wheat that gives bread structure. Gluten free baking uses alternative flours like rice, almond, coconut, or tapioca flour. Common substitutions include buttermilk made by adding vinegar or lemon juice to milk, applesauce or mashed banana for oil or eggs in baking, Greek yogurt for sour cream, and honey or maple syrup for sugar but reduce other liquid. Kitchen equipment essentials include chef's knife, cutting board, measuring cups and spoons, mixing bowls, skillet, saucepan, stockpot, baking sheet, baking dish, colander, and spatula. Common cuisines include Italian with pasta, pizza, risotto, and tomato sauce, Mexican with tacos, enchiladas, guacamole, and salsa, Chinese with stir fry, dumplings, fried rice, and noodles, Indian with curry, naan, lentil dal, and spices, Japanese with sushi, ramen, tempura, and teriyaki, French with sauces like béchamel and hollandaise, pastries, and coq au vin, American with burgers, barbecue, apple pie, and macaroni and cheese. People ask about travel. Passports and visas are required for international travel. Passports take weeks to process. Expedited service is faster but costs more. Visa requirements vary by country. Travel insurance covers trip cancellation, medical emergencies, lost luggage, and evacuation. Jet lag is caused by crossing time zones quickly. Symptoms include fatigue, insomnia, and digestive issues. To reduce jet lag, adjust sleep schedule before travel, get sunlight exposure at destination, stay hydrated, and avoid alcohol and caffeine. Travel safety includes researching destinations, keeping copies of documents, not flashing valuables, being aware of surroundings, avoiding unsafe areas at night, and having local emergency numbers. Common travel scams include fake petitions, taxi overcharging, broken meter, the dropped bracelet, free gifts that are not free, and rental scams. To avoid pickpocketing, use a money belt, keep bags zipped and in front, and do not keep wallet in back pocket. Road trip tips include checking vehicle maintenance, planning route with rest stops, packing snacks and water, having paper maps in case of no cell service, taking breaks every two hours, and sharing driving when possible. People ask about money and personal finance. Budgeting methods include the 50 30 20 rule, 50 percent for needs like housing and food, 30 percent for wants like entertainment and dining out, 20 percent for savings and debt repayment. Zero based budgeting assigns every dollar a purpose. Envelope budgeting uses cash in envelopes for different spending categories. Emergency fund should cover 3 to 6 months of expenses. Save for emergencies before investing. Saving money tips include automating transfers to savings, reducing subscriptions, cooking at home, using public libraries, buying used items, comparing prices, and avoiding impulse purchases. Checking accounts are for everyday spending. Savings accounts earn interest but have withdrawal limits. High yield savings accounts pay higher interest online. Certificates of deposit, or CDs, lock money for a fixed time for higher interest. Money market accounts are hybrids. Credit cards allow borrowing up to a limit, with interest if not paid in full. Paying the full balance every month avoids interest. Credit utilization below 30 percent of limit helps credit score. Debit cards pull directly from checking accounts. Credit scores range from 300 to 850. FICO score factors include payment history at 35 percent, amounts owed at 30 percent, length of credit history at 15 percent, new credit at 10 percent, and credit mix at 10 percent. Higher scores get better loan rates. To improve credit, pay bills on time, reduce debt, keep old accounts open, and check credit reports annually for free. Loans include mortgages for homes, auto loans for cars, student loans for education, and personal loans for any purpose. Interest rates are annual percentage rate or APR. Fixed rates stay the same. Variable rates change. Secured loans have collateral like a house or car. Unsecured loans do not. Compound interest is interest earned on interest. It grows investments exponentially over time. The rule of 72 estimates years to double money by dividing 72 by annual interest rate. For 8 percent interest, 72 divided by 8 equals 9 years to double. Investing includes stocks, which are shares of ownership in companies. Bonds are loans to companies or governments that pay interest. Mutual funds pool money from many investors to buy a portfolio of stocks and bonds. Exchange traded funds or ETFs are like mutual funds but trade like stocks. Index funds track an index like the S and P 500. Diversification spreads investments across different assets to reduce risk. Risk tolerance is the ability to handle market downturns. Dollar cost averaging invests fixed amounts regularly regardless of price. Retirement accounts include 401k through employers, often with matching contributions, and IRA or individual retirement account. Roth accounts are funded with after tax money, withdrawals in retirement are tax free. Traditional accounts are funded with pre tax money, withdrawals are taxed as income. Social Security provides retirement income in the United States, funded by payroll taxes. Full retirement age is 67 for those born after 1960. Claiming early at 62 reduces benefits. Delaying past full retirement age increases benefits. Taxes include income tax, which is progressive, higher income pays higher rate. Payroll tax funds Social Security and Medicare. Sales tax is added to purchases. Property tax is on real estate. Capital gains tax is on investment profits. Short term capital gains, for assets held less than one year, are taxed as ordinary income. Long term capital gains, for assets held more than one year, have lower rates. Tax deductions reduce taxable income. The standard deduction is a fixed amount. Itemized deductions include mortgage interest, charitable donations, and medical expenses. Tax credits directly reduce tax owed. The earned income tax credit helps low income workers. Child tax credit helps parents. Filing tax returns is due April 15 in the United States. Extensions are available. Debt repayment strategies include the avalanche method, paying highest interest rate debt first to save most money, and the snowball method, paying smallest balance first for psychological wins. Consolidation combines multiple debts into one loan. Refinancing replaces a loan with a new one at lower interest rate. Bankruptcy is a legal process for overwhelming debt. Chapter 7 liquidates assets to pay debts. Chapter 13 creates a repayment plan. Bankruptcy stays on credit reports for 7 to 10 years. People ask about law and government. The United States has three branches of government. The legislative branch, Congress, makes laws. Congress has two houses, the Senate with 100 senators, two per state, and the House of Representatives with 435 members based on population. The executive branch, the President, enforces laws. The judicial branch, the Supreme Court and lower courts, interprets laws. The Constitution is the supreme law of the United States. The Bill of Rights is the first ten amendments. The First Amendment protects freedom of speech, press, religion, assembly, and petition. The Second Amendment protects the right to bear arms. The Fourth Amendment protects against unreasonable search and seizure. The Fifth Amendment protects against self incrimination and double jeopardy. The Sixth Amendment guarantees right to a speedy and public trial, impartial jury, and legal counsel. The Eighth Amendment prohibits cruel and unusual punishment. The Fourteenth Amendment guarantees equal protection under the law and due process. Criminal law deals with crimes against the state. Felonies are serious crimes like murder, rape, robbery, and burglary, punishable by more than one year in prison. Misdemeanors are less serious like petty theft, simple assault, and disorderly conduct, punishable by less than one year in jail. Infractions are minor like traffic tickets, punishable by fines. Criminal procedure includes arrest, booking, arraignment where charges are read and plea entered, pretrial motions, trial, verdict, and sentencing. The burden of proof is beyond a reasonable doubt. Civil law deals with disputes between individuals or organizations. Lawsuits involve a plaintiff who brings the case and a defendant who responds. Common civil cases include contract disputes, property disputes, personal injury, and family law like divorce and child custody. The burden of proof is preponderance of the evidence, more likely than not. Contracts are legally binding agreements. They require offer, acceptance, consideration which is something of value exchanged, mutual assent, and legal purpose. Breach of contract occurs when a party fails to fulfill their obligations. Torts are civil wrongs not arising from contract. Intentional torts include assault, battery, false imprisonment, defamation, and fraud. Negligence occurs when someone fails to exercise reasonable care, causing harm. The elements of negligence are duty, breach, causation, and damages. Property law includes real property or land and buildings, and personal property or movable items. Lease agreements for renting property specify rent, duration, and responsibilities of landlord and tenant. Employment law covers minimum wage, overtime pay, workplace safety under OSHA, anti discrimination under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act which protects against discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, and national origin. The Americans with Disabilities Act, or ADA, prohibits discrimination against people with disabilities. Family law includes marriage, divorce, child custody, child support, alimony, and adoption. Divorce can be no fault, where neither party is blamed, or fault based, where one party is blamed for adultery, cruelty, or abandonment.
